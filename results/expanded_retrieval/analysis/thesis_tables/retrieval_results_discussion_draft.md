# Draft thesis text: expanded retrieval evaluation

## Results: expanded retrieval evaluation

The expanded retrieval evaluation used the SciFact, NFCorpus, and ArguAna BEIR test collections. The combined corpus contained 17,490 documents and the evaluation included 2,024 test queries with at least one relevant document available in the corresponding corpus. All available official relevance judgements were retained, except that five ArguAna judgements whose document identifiers were absent from the released corpus could not enter metric denominators. Results were calculated separately for each collection; they were not pooled across domains.

Four chunking strategies were evaluated: fixed-size, sentence-based, semantic, and topic-aware chunking. Documents were ranked by the maximum similarity score of their constituent chunks, using a common MiniLM embedding model and exact inner-product search. The evaluation reports hit rate, recall, precision, reciprocal rank, and nDCG at cut-offs 1, 3, 5, and 10. The complete descriptive results are shown in Table 1 [insert `descriptive_all_cutoffs.csv` or its formatted equivalent here].

The descriptive results vary by collection. On SciFact, all strategies retrieved a relevant document for approximately 0.80--0.81 of queries at rank 10; topic-aware chunking had the highest descriptive nDCG at rank 10 (0.671), followed closely by semantic chunking (0.666). On NFCorpus, semantic and topic-aware chunking had higher descriptive precision and nDCG than fixed-size chunking at the deeper cut-offs. For example, at rank 10, precision/nDCG were 0.224/0.298 for fixed-size chunking, 0.240/0.317 for sentence-based chunking, 0.246/0.324 for semantic chunking, and 0.241/0.321 for topic-aware chunking. On ArguAna, descriptive differences were small and did not show a consistent ordering across cut-offs.

Pairwise inference used the query as the paired unit. For every dataset, metric, cut-off, and unordered strategy pair, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Pratt handling of zeros was calculated and Holm-adjusted over the full family of 360 comparisons. Mean paired differences (first strategy minus second strategy), 95% percentile-bootstrap confidence intervals based on 10,000 resamples, and signed rank-biserial correlations were also reported. Table 2 [insert `holm_significant_comparisons.csv` or its formatted equivalent here] lists the nine comparisons that remained statistically significant after Holm adjustment.

All nine Holm-significant comparisons occurred on NFCorpus. Relative to semantic chunking, fixed-size chunking had lower precision at ranks 3, 5, and 10 (mean differences -0.040, -0.032, and -0.022, respectively), and lower nDCG at rank 10 (-0.025). Relative to sentence-based chunking, fixed-size chunking had lower precision at rank 10 (-0.015). The adjusted tests also identified differences in recall between fixed-size and semantic chunking at ranks 3, 5, and 10, and between fixed-size and sentence-based chunking at rank 10. The bootstrap confidence intervals for the three fixed-size versus semantic recall mean differences crossed zero, so these particular recall findings should be reported as a difference detected by the signed-rank test rather than as unambiguous evidence of a non-zero mean recall change. The fixed-size versus sentence-based recall difference at rank 10 was -0.010 (95% CI -0.019 to -0.003).

No comparison on SciFact or ArguAna remained statistically significant after the stated Holm correction. This result is evidence that the study did not detect a difference under this design and correction; it is not evidence that the strategies are equivalent.

## Discussion: interpretation and limits of the retrieval findings

The expanded evaluation does not support a single chunking strategy as universally superior. The evidence is collection-specific: on NFCorpus, the strongest supported pattern is lower fixed-size precision at ranks 3--10 and lower fixed-size nDCG at rank 10 when compared with semantic chunking, with a smaller precision and recall disadvantage against sentence-based chunking at rank 10. On SciFact and ArguAna, the observed descriptive ordering did not survive family-wise error control. These results therefore support a conditional interpretation: the effect of chunking depends on the retrieval collection and rank cut-off examined.

The conservative adjustment is important because the analysis evaluated multiple strategies, metrics, cut-offs, and collections. Reporting unadjusted p-values alone would overstate the evidence. Conversely, the absence of an adjusted significant result must not be interpreted as proof that two strategies retrieve equivalently; the study was designed to test for detectable differences, not to establish an equivalence margin.

The results are retrieval findings, not direct evidence of generated-answer correctness. The pipeline retrieves chunks but evaluates document relevance after aggregating chunk scores by the maximum score for each document. Consequently, the reported metrics show whether the parent document is ranked highly; they do not directly measure whether the exact supporting passage was retrieved. Chunk counts also differ across strategies, but this experiment did not isolate computational efficiency or use chunk count as a causal explanation for effectiveness differences.

The separate RAGAS evaluation should be reported as a complementary, descriptive answer-level assessment. It includes faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision, and context recall with a documented local evaluator model, but it does not provide a validated answer-correctness measure. Claims about response quality should therefore be limited to the RAGAS metrics actually obtained, including their parsing failures and use of partial results, rather than framed as proof of factual answer correctness.
